\reportpage{04 / DISTANCE, DENSITY AND TIMESCALE}{Are we entering a different neighbourhood?}
Many disk stars share Galactic rotation but have different three-dimensional velocities. Their \emph{relative} motions determine encounters. Radius from the Galactic centre and height above the plane describe different aspects of the environment; neither alone determines an individual star's speed.

\subsection*{Density is not a discovery count}
A historical census of 373 stars within 10 pc [5] sets the local reference:
\[
 n_0=\frac{373}{(4\pi/3)(10\unit{pc})^3}
     =0.0890\unit{pc^{-3}}.
\]
The adopted thin/thick-disk scenario [6] ranges over \textbf{0.784--1.063} times its initial density in 250 Myr: about \textbf{292--397 stars} in an instantaneous 10 pc sphere. It omits spiral-arm enhancements, clusters and migration. These are neither distinct arrivals nor planets found.
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[t]{.485\linewidth}\vspace{0pt}\setlength{\parskip}{3pt}
\subsection*{Proxima is approaching now}
The adopted historical inputs [7] give about \textbf{4.24 ly} in 2026 and a radial velocity of \textbf{$-22.19\unit{km\,s^{-1}}$}; distance is decreasing. The total relative speed is about $32.51\unit{km\,s^{-1}}$.

Straight-line propagation gives a minimum near \textbf{3.10 ly after 26,700 years}. This omits the Alpha Centauri system's orbital dynamics and Galactic differential acceleration. A negative radial speed today does not imply permanent approach.
\end{minipage}

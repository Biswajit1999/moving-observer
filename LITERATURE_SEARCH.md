# Literature scope and contribution assessment

Search date: 24 October 2026. Author of project: Biswajit Jana.

Question: Does a fixed nearby-star sample show a robust change in direct-imaging accessibility over a century when instrument thresholds are held fixed, and how does that change interact with an improved instrument?

Web searches included: `exoplanet direct imaging yield stellar proper motion future detectability changing viewpoint century`; `"proper motion" "yield" "direct imaging" exoplanet future`; `"stellar motion" "detectability" exoplanets future`; `"exoplanet" "proper motion" "completeness" "future"`; `"direct imaging" "stellar motion" "yield"`; `"exoplanet" "century" "detectability"`; `"exoplanet" "counterfactual" "motion"`. This was a targeted web and primary-source search, not an exhaustive bibliometric review. Some papers were examined at abstract/method-summary level; no claim is made that every full text or citation chain was surveyed.

| Primary work | Established overlap | Difference from the current experiment |
|---|---|---|
| [Rafikov 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.0008) | Proper motion changes planetary transit geometry/timing | Transit timing, not this single-visit direct-imaging threshold comparison |
| [Kaltenegger & Faherty 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07936) | Moving stars enter/leave Earth-transit viewing opportunities | Reciprocal Earth-transit visibility over millennia |
| [Bailer-Jones 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.06258) | Gaia phase-space propagation, stellar encounters and uncertainty | Close encounters, not a telescope accessibility score |
| [Stark et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.5128), [2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.11988) | Direct-imaging completeness, instrument dependence, target selection and yields | Much more realistic mission modelling than this project's threshold proxy |
| [Savransky & Garrett / EXOSIMS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02869) | End-to-end mission simulation and synthetic planet populations | Appropriate foundation for future scheduled-yield work |
| [Latouf et al. 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25540) | Yield modelling and survey-strategy comparisons | Recent preprint; not evidence that telescope technology can be forecast for 2126 |
| [Lauer et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21666) | Separated observers and stellar parallax with New Horizons | Demonstrates a useful baseline without a stationary probe |

The search did not identify an exact duplicate of this particular dated sample, paired four-scenario calculation and IWA sign-sensitivity test. That is not proof of novelty. Do not describe the work as the first demonstration that moving viewpoints change detectability, or as deeper than established mission-yield studies. The current contribution is a reproducible exploratory comparison and a falsifiable follow-up question. Expert review may identify closer precedents.

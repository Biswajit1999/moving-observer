# Discussion brief

**Biswajit Jana · Finding Earth 2.0**

I am exploring how relative motion between the Sun and other stars could change our ability to detect or characterize exoplanets. Which changes in distance and viewing direction could create a meaningful observing opportunity?

I have prepared an interactive discussion model and a report with equations, assumptions and prior references:

- Simulation: https://biswajit1999.github.io/moving-observer/
- Report: https://biswajit1999.github.io/moving-observer/Report.html
- Reproducible source and feedback: https://github.com/Biswajit1999/moving-observer

The model distinguishes fixed-reference displacement, relative stellar motion and method-specific detectability. It includes a simplified Proxima approach calculation and explicitly synthetic long-term targets. It does not claim a new physical principle, new planet, habitability result or precision encounter forecast. Transit-zone and stellar-encounter literature is cited.

I would appreciate references to existing answers, corrections, or suggestions for a first useful catalogue-and-instrument case study. Which observable could change appreciably with instrument capability held fixed, and what uncertainty analysis would demonstrate that?
